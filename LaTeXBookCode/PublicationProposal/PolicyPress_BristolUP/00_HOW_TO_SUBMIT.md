# Policy Press / Bristol University Press — how to submit

**Route:** email to a named subject editor. There is no web form and no central submissions inbox.
**Send to:** **Paul Stevens**, Publisher (Social Research Methods), `paul.stevens@bristol.ac.uk`
**Their pages:** [Submitting your proposal](https://bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/author-hub/submitting-your-proposal) · [Social Research Methods subject page](https://policy.bristoluniversitypress.co.uk/social-research-methods)

## Consider emailing him first

Their page invites an informal conversation before formal submission, and in this case it is worth taking. Paul Stevens took over their social research methods list in 2022 and writes on the subject page that he is *"currently working to expand our catalogue in this area,"* that they are *"expanding our catalogue to cover the full range of approaches,"* and that *"of particular interest are books that are concerned with methodology, as well as skills- and practice-based books."* Their existing methods list is heavily qualitative and creative-methods. A short note asking whether a quantitative, practice-based Stata book is in scope will get you a fast read, and if the answer is yes the full proposal lands warm.

`01_cover_letter_paul_stevens.md` works either as that opening note or as the covering email for the full package.

## A note on length

The filled proposal runs **11 pages**, against their stated guideline of 5–8 excluding CVs and samples. That is deliberate, and their own requirements are why: section 4 asks for a chapter-by-chapter summary of **200–300 words per chapter**, and with fifteen chapters that single section would be 3,000–4,500 words, which alone exceeds eight pages. My chapter summaries run about 120–160 words each, already well under what they ask, and every other section is tight.

So the two instructions cannot both be met by a fifteen-chapter book. I chose to follow the per-chapter requirement and overshoot the page count, since the chapter summaries are what an editor actually reads to judge the book. If you would rather hit the page guidance, the section to cut is 4: reduce each chapter to a single sentence and offer the full summaries on request. Say the word and I will produce that version.

## Format category: say "Learning Resources"

They require the book to fit one of six declared formats: Research Monographs, Multi-author/edited works, **Learning Resources**, Policy and Practice, Trade, or Shorts. This is a Learning Resource, and saying so unprompted signals you have read their guidelines. It also triggers the **textbook annex on page 6** of their guidelines, which asks about pedagogical features and companion digital content. That annex is answered in section 11 of my proposal, and it is where the runnable do-files and package suite do the most work.

## The one section only you can complete

**Section 10, Referees.** They ask for **at least four** suitably qualified people, with contact details, and they explicitly ask that the list be diverse and include scholars at Global South institutions. I have left this as a structured blank with guidance on the kinds of reviewer to name. I have deliberately not invented names or email addresses. Supplying real people's contact details is your call to make, and a fabricated list would be caught immediately.

## What happens next, and when

- **Initial conversation** with the subject editor. No stated duration.
- **Peer review of the proposal:** they commit to collating responses and getting back to you *"within 6–8 weeks of receiving your proposal."*
- **A Publishing Committee / BUP Board meeting** makes the contract decision. No stated duration.
- **Second peer review** of the full typescript after delivery. Because your manuscript is finished, this gate arrives sooner than it would for most proposals, an advantage on speed, but it means the book gets reviewed largely as-is rather than shaped around reviewer comments.
- **Production:** their milestone list runs from final typescript delivery six months before publication back through copy-editing, typesetting, proofreading, and indexing, with cover work starting ten to eleven months out. Roughly **11–12 months from delivered typescript to publication**, though that is inferred from their milestone list rather than a figure they state.

Tell them if the proposal is under consideration elsewhere, since their guidelines specifically invite this so they can move faster.

## Cost

None on the standard route. Nothing on their submission page, their why-publish page, or the proposal guidelines mentions a fee, subvention, or author contribution. Section 12 of their template asks whether you have open-access funding or an OA mandate; the honest answer is no, and that is not a problem.

## Honest read on fit

This is the best **audience** match of the three presses. Policy Press exists for applied social policy and practitioner readers, they already publish Helen Kara's *Research and Evaluation for Busy Students and Practitioners*, and their template's textbook annex explicitly asks about companion datasets and student exercises, which is exactly what this book has and most methods books do not.

The weakness is the **software** precedent. Their only statistical-software title I could find is Cole Davis's *SPSS Step by Step* from 2013, there is no Stata book anywhere on their list, and the current methods catalogue is overwhelmingly qualitative. That is why the letter leads with the editor's own stated expansion plan rather than with Stata.
